Data: vsa_klines (gap-flagged bars) + true_volume (proxy stack) + large_trades (Tier-3) + volume_profile (tape by price) + checkonchain_urpd (on-chain supply by cost basis + holder cohorts). OHLC merge rule: spot bars to 2024-12-31 (spot 1d extended via flagged cross-fills), then futures UM to the latest refresh. Every volume number carries its source + confidence tier on hover.
Right-edge profile: bars = traded notional per price level over the selected window (Binance USD-M futures tape, one venue) — a level where real volume actually changed hands.
Cost basis (on-chain URPD — supply bucketed by the price its owner paid) shows who is selling and at what cost, by comparing two dates. Same right edge as the tape profile, drawn behind it: the hollow outline is how much supply sat at that price on the earlier date, the filled bar is how much sits there on the later one, and the cap between the two ends is the change — green where the cohort grew (coins parked at that cost basis), red where it shrank (that cohort's coins moved). All three share one scale, so a cap twice as long is twice the BTC — but they are a different measure from the tape bars in front of them and are not comparable bar-for-bar. Hover a level for its two dates and the change, and the Who Moved card below ranks the biggest sellers and buyers with the price they bought at, tagged above/below the price at the end of the window: a cohort spent above its own cost basis is realised profit, below is realised loss.
The date pair beside the cost-basis window buttons asks for any window at all: pick two dates and it subtracts the supply curves stored for them. Those curves come from a weekly archive (checkonchain's own series API, one date a week since 2016), so each end snaps to the nearest stored date within a few days and the status strip says which two dates it actually answered with. The newest archive dates are still being restated upstream, so a window ending within about a month of today is provisional; nothing before 2016-01-03 is available at all. A pair is served on the archive's own $200 price grid, finer than the published windows — which is why the Who Moved card still groups its rows into $1,000 zones, so one wall of owners is one row rather than ten fragments.
The ETF flows bars in the candle pane (above the volume histogram, toggle with the eye beside the label) are the daily net flows of the US spot BTC ETFs, in USD — creations minus redemptions, one bar per calendar day since the first session on 2024-01-11, green for a net inflow and red for a net outflow. That series is daily and session-dated: a weekend or holiday carries an explicit 0, which means the US market did not open — not a day with no flow — and the newest day is provisional until upstream restates it. Dollars, not BTC, so do not measure it against the BTC-denominated layers.
Cost-basis and holder-cohort data comes from checkonchain's live charts and is current to the vintage stamped on each panel. The 14-day window is gone — the publisher retired it. Every preset window is the publisher's own subtraction except 4y, which it never built: that one is computed here from two archived curves, and its earlier curve ends below today's price range, so the zones above that line read as accumulation since rather than a like-for-like pair (the status strip names the line). It is real per-UTXO data computed by checkonchain's node, so it is a dated measurement, not the live tape beside it.
